title: Differential pathway scores
language: R
summary: Tests which pathway scores differ between groups, picking the right test from the number of groups: limma for two, Kruskal-Wallis with post-hoc for more.

Takes a pathways-by-samples score matrix and tests every row for a difference
between groups. The test is chosen from your data rather than configured:

**Two groups** get limma's moderated t-test. It is worth the dependency
because it shrinks each pathway's variance towards the average across all
pathways - with a handful of samples per group, that is the difference between
a usable ranking and noise.

**Three or more groups** get Kruskal-Wallis, then Dunn's post-hoc on whatever
survives. The post-hoc step is the one usually skipped, and it is the one that
answers the actual question: not whether the groups differ but *which pair*
does.

Non-parametric on purpose for the multi-group case - ssGSEA scores are bounded
and not normally distributed, and with small groups an ANOVA's assumptions are
unverifiable rather than merely unverified.

## Not only ssGSEA

Any score matrix fits: protein abundance, metabolite panels, cell-type
deconvolution fractions, methylation summaries. Rows are features, columns are
samples.

## When sample names do not match

Pipelines rewrite sample names - dashes become dots, suffixes get appended -
and the score matrix and the group sheet then disagree. A configurable pattern
rewrites both sides before matching, defaulting to no rewriting at all.

## Requirements

R - 4.2 or newer
limma - the two-group moderated t-test
FSA - Dunn's post-hoc test for three or more groups
ggplot2 - the box plots
pheatmap - the heatmap

## How to run it

```r
install.packages(c("ggplot2", "pheatmap", "FSA"))
BiocManager::install("limma")
```

Then:

```sh
Rscript pathway_score_testing.R
```

Writes a results table, the post-hoc pairs where relevant, a box-plot panel of
the most significant pathways and a heatmap.
